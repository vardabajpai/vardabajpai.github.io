<p>2014 Toyota Corolla, Grey, 76k miles. Clean title, no check engine lights. Asking 10k (read: anything over 8k).</p><p></p><p>Not listed:</p><p>I was eight years old when my parents bought this car. I used to like the Prius more— likely because the Prius took me to art classes and the Corolla took me to Kumon. Then we sold the Prius, and the Corolla slowly traded its nauseating new-car smell for the scent of spilt chai and fries routinely vacuumed out from the crevices of the back right seat, where I had an equal view of the road and the expressions on the corner of the driver’s face.</p><p>The longest drives were always to family in Pennsylvania: 4 hours, 5 with traffic. I would lie down in the back seat and watch the clouds pull the sun away to night. This was pre-iPad and roaming data, so the only other entertainment available to watch was the raindrops collecting on the car’s window. I assigned them names and placed bets with myself on which droplet would swell enough to burst first. When there was no precipitation, I would watch the other cars on the highway, and if I was lucky, the Corolla would pull us past a semi-truck at just the right speed where the driver could see the small, hopeful double-pump of my arm and oblige me with a responsive honk of his horn. I spent most of these journeys rolling around the backseat of the car, thinking about all the things I would say and do with my best friends when I saw them at the end of this arduous journey. Sometimes, when especially sympathetic to my boredom, the car would gently fill my ears and lull me to sleep with the Greatest Top 100 Hits of the Year playing steadily through the inbuilt stereo.</p><p>While incredibly understanding in times of crisis, the Corolla also quickly established itself as a dependable friend. Consistently, the car dropped me off at school in the morning and picked me up in the afternoon. She endured fits of anger and boredom unleashed, and slowly soaked in all of the day’s gossip. She moved towns when I did, and heard new names from a new school. She met new friends who joined me in the back seat after a long hard day of pre-teen bullshit and 8am-3pm arithmetic, and greeted their parents’ cars in the driveway when it was time for them to go back home.</p><p>--</p><p>I lost around two years of my life to COVID, and as I rotted, gathering dust in the confines of my room, the Corolla rotted just a floor underneath me, tucked away in the garage only to be let out for the occasional biweekly grocery run. Both of us likely have no recollection of the time. Then, 16. I spent weeks in online drivers ed classes before I was finally, painstakingly, allowed to practice from the drivers seat. I will not lie and say that I learned on the Corolla-- it was deemed that the newer Honda Pilot was more likely to withstand whatever worst-case scenario played in my parents' minds. However, after weeks of too-wide right turns and being told "I drove like I was afraid of the brakes", it was the Corolla that took me to my test, and the Corolla that accompanied me on my victorious one-hour drive back. And eventually, out of an equal mix of my mother's aversion to the road and my family's natural inclinations towards our vehicles (I found that I couldn't weave lanes in the Pilot and my dad felt cramped in the Corolla), the car's keys became permanently stashed in my backpack.</p><p>It is not even slightly an understatement to say that being given those keys was like being given my life back. When I tell people that I don't remember much of the pandemic, the actuality is that my brain has, against my will, almost entirely wiped itself of any recollection of the time between when lockdown was announced in my eighth grade history class to the day that I was handed the keys to that car. The Corolla took me to the worst junior prom of my life and the best drive I've ever experienced on the way back. She let me pile far too many people into the backseat so we could volunteer at a local farm (or change plans and go to McDonalds) after school. She slowly physically changed as our personalities meshed, starting with the stupidest bumper sticker I could find plastered to the back and ending with the smell of my perfume, so pervasive that I doubt any cleaning will ever really be able to get it out completely. She witnessed the pure euphoria that overtook me when I heard my favorite song for the first time. She heard that song many times. She took me to my best friends' houses, one time in Maine. She popped a tire once, about 5 minutes from my house, and it took 3 hours and an elementary school teacher wearing cowboy boots to get the donut on. I have so many stories with that lovely car that they sometimes spill out of me when I see her dopplegangers on the road. For now, I will leave that irritation for my friends and refrain from cramming them all into this eulogy.</p><p>The day that we sold her, everyone I had ever loved deeply had spent time in her passenger seat. I had a funeral for my car. It sounds ridiculous, and it was almost definitely partially an excuse to get my friends together so we could drink on my patio. In my mind, there was no more fitting conclusion for her journey than cramming the people I love together with music and laughter and nowhere to go but somewhere to be.</p><p>When I speak about that car, I am sometimes asked why I never named her. Admittedly, it often feels like it is hard to convey the depth of my relationship with my first car if I never even took the time to give her a name that wasn't assigned at the dealership and didn't belong to thousands of others. But I like to think that if she could speak, she never had any nicknames for me either. Instead, we had music that was way too loud or complete silence (because the Bluetooth system stopped working). She witnessed every emotion I have ever experienced in all of its teenage irrationality and hormonal abundance. She silently supported conversations that should and will never be heard again. She did what I can only hope to do for the world-- she let me be and was with me.</p><p></p><p>2014 Toyota Corolla, Grey, 76k miles. Clean title, messy teenage girl who will always live within me. You big plastic vehicle. Thank you, and I miss you, and I love you. I hope the road treats you as well as you treated me.</p>
