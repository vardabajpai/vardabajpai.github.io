<p>August 14, 2025, at 11:17 PM</p><p></p><p>Tonight is one of those nights I spend going through old photos and feeling a great deal of gratitude for the capacity of love I receive and give. Unfortunately, I am not inebriated enough to acceptably express all of my thoughts to my friends without setting off a few "is she about to drink cyanide or go for a walk on the highway" alarms, so I figured now's as good a time as any to write.</p><p></p><p>Since this is the first thing I'm actually uploading to this page, I would like to disclaim (to the room of empty chairs reading this): nothing about this piece, or anything I write, is more polished than a copy and paste from my notes app. Do not expect literature, or a broad, expansive worldview. For the most part, I still think I'm a child. I haven't read enough literature or philosophy to present anything truly deep, so everything I write will be strictly from my experience. This experience is limited; I grew up in Connecticut and have been on vacation a few times, but I have never really traveled. I have let my brain sit on a very high shelf for many years, and I am now beginning the process of dusting it off; writing is a part of that. I'm aware of the possibility that I will read this again, 10 years or 10 days from now, and I will laugh at what a silly girl I was for thinking the way that I do.  I don't know much about anything, but I am very lucky nevertheless, because I think I know quite a bit about love. If this is my field of expertise, it is because am blessed with wonderful friends who taught me everything I know. Here are some of the important parts.</p><div><hr></div><p>I will always strive to put enough love out into the universe that if even a quarter of it is returned, my cup will overflow. I deeply believe that you will not get anywhere with love expecting that every bit you give will be received in kind. Part of this is because you will not even comprehend half of the love that is sent your way. It'll be written to you in languages you won't speak until years later, when you realize that your best friend in 6th grade gave you her favorite painting from the art class you took together because she was trying to tell you that she would give her life for yours.</p><p>Others will not always understand your language, either. You'll spend your last few dollars from your summer job ordering food because your best friend offhandedly said they were hungry. You may feel conflicted in this moment. You won't want your friend to feel guilty about your now sub-zero bank balance, but you also want them to understand the depth of their importance to you. You will feel, selfishly, that your love is at risk of going unappreciated. You are not a saint. This is human nature. In these moments, it is important to recognize is that your love cannot ever go unappreciated if you are there for yourself to appreciate it. An act of kindness is not negated by any associated positive feelings of self-appreciation. Arguably, even if acts of charity are incentivized by a sense of satisfaction, the recipients of this charity will not take offense. Rather, they may prefer if this satisfaction evolves into drunken euphoria-- something to be chased after and sought out night after night until everyone surrounding the love junkie has experienced their quiet, selfishly-driven generosity in invisible ways. I believe there are much worse things to become addicted to than kindness.</p><p>Sometimes you will hand your love off to the wrong people. You will write stories and songs for girls who call you mean names behind your back, and it'll hurt you when you find out but eventually you'll realize that the love wasn't wasted-- because love never is. Anyways, I would much rather commit the crime of loving those who don't deserve it than hoard my love like it is a nonrenewable resource in finite supply. In truth, and in the least (and most) <em>My Little Pony</em>-derived sense, the solution to the metaphysical energy crisis is love. But I digress.</p><p></p><p>Here is a short story for you. One warm day in June, you will leave your high school graduation, streaming out in a sea of people you spent your childhood growing up with, despising, learning from, and loving. You will cry and laugh while the air fills with cigar smoke, and you'll take pictures with the girl who called you a slut once and brought a cupcake to class for you because she noticed when you threw out your lunch for weeks. You will be surrounded by all of the correct and incorrect recipients of your love-- sometimes within the same soul-- and the lines will begin to blur until you will no longer be able to distinguish between the faces you despised and admired. That moment-- when the lines fade away entirely and your heart fills until it is brimming-- will fill you with an unimaginable exhilaration. You'll see it in your eyes when you look back at the photos. Your heart will be as open as it has ever been, and it won't matter if in your excitement, you congratulate a boy who bullied you; because your love is not for, or about, anyone else. Love and expectation and entirely contradictory, and love given is never misplaced. Sure, you can look back at your past and regret the time you wasted surrounded by the wrong people, giving love where it was not seen or wanted. But love, unlike time, can never be wasted-- and anyways, time spent loving is time well spent, so even that evens out in the end.</p><p>At my high school graduation, amidst the cigar smoke and pure euphoria that filled the air, I let myself become so consumed by this feeling that I forgot to distinguish who I loved and who I didn't. This is when I learned what I now believe to be truth-- humans are hardwired to have love for everyone, and in its purest moments, a soul will connect with every stranger and friend to cross its path. My friends showed up with balloons and bouquets and a terribly chosen picture of my face plastered onto an egregiously large cardboard sign. My friends showed up with all of their love, and it filled my cup a thousand times over, and my gratitude poured out to them, as well as everyone around me. I pulled flowers from the bouquets my friends had chosen for me and handed them out to my favorite teachers, and the parking lot staff who I had never seen before a day in my life, and the statistics teacher who personally victimized me for a year. It didn’t matter who it was; my gratitude for the love my friends had passed off to me poured out to everyone around me within shouting distance.</p><p>In retrospect, it was incredibly important to recognize that the kindness and love that I was handing out like flowers didn’t just come from within me. This was almost critical-- as much as you appreciate the love you give, you must maintain equal, if not greater, reverence for the love you are given. You will not always feel loved. There will be days or weeks where you convince yourself that everyone in your life is there out of pity, or you'll forget to recognize that anyone is there at all. It is imperative that you take a moment to fill your cup entirely in moments where others are able to speak your language and you feel entirely and completely loved. It is also imperative to pour into your cup even if you only understand two words of the sentence spoken to you, because those two words may be spoken in a different language, and it may not be the most effective form of communication, but nevertheless it is proof that you are loved. If I were to fall off the planet tomorrow and drift off into space, never to see any of my friends again, I will remember that I was loved. If I had not breathed in the moment entirely on that day in June, I would easily have forgotten. Thank your friends for loving you in whatever ways you can, because whatever you give them does not necessitate a reciprocal response. As harsh as it may sound, love is not transactional or owed to you, and it will never be. If the love you give is granted without expectation of reciprocity, it will taste ten times as sweet when it is returned to you. This is admittedly much easier said than done.</p><div><hr></div><p>I am now realizing that this writing is not being graded, which means I don't have to conclude it properly or proofread, or find a synonym for the word "love" to eliminate the inevitable redundancy I'm sure you've already noticed(, empty chair). These are not even close to all of my thoughts; maybe next time I’ll structure them better. Goodnight. I hope you let your love consume you tomorrow, and every day after that.</p><p></p><p>August 15, 2025, at 1:26 AM</p>
